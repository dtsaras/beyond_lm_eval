# Topology Tasks

This module contains metrics that use Topological Data Analysis (TDA) to describe the shape of hidden-state point clouds across model layers.

**Registry coverage (5 tasks)**: `topology_betti_curve`,
`topology_homology`, `topology_persistence_entropy`,
`topology_persistence_landscape`, and `topology_zigzag_persistence`.

**Point clouds**: `topology_homology`, `topology_persistence_entropy`,
`topology_persistence_landscape` and `topology_betti_curve` read one point per
sequence: the last token of a 20-word window cut from each passage at a hashed
offset (`src/blme/tasks/_windows.py`), the same words for every model, with
`num_samples: 1000` sequences. Whole passages are opt-in
(`points_readout: passage`). `topology_zigzag_persistence` follows the
Gardinazzi et al. protocol instead (last token of each prompt, tokenised
without special tokens). All tasks require `ripser`; zigzag also requires
`dionysus`.

**Citations**: Persistence landscapes cite Bubenik's original JMLR/arXiv paper
(`arXiv:1207.6437`); `arXiv:1501.00179` is the later Bubenik-Dlotko toolbox
paper, an implementation reference and not the original landscape definition.

## 1. Persistent Homology & Betti-0
* **What are we measuring**: The fundamental topological connectivity of the semantic latent space.
* **How are we measuring**: Vietoris-Rips persistence (`ripser`, dimensions 0 and 1, float64 pairwise distances) on one point per sequence (the last token of a 20-word window of each passage) at the first, middle and last block, with `num_samples: 1000` for every model. H0/H1 lifetimes are summarised by their mean and maximum and by the H1 bar count. Bars are also split into fine and coarse at a death threshold of 0.5 x the cloud's mean pairwise distance (counts and the fine share of persistence mass); this split is a BLME construct motivated by Fay et al. (arXiv:2505.20435).
* **Hypothesis**: Random noise text will form a massive disconnected point cloud. Mathematically coherent language will collapse into deeply connected low-dimensional sub-manifolds, reflecting rule-based syntax.
* **Citation/Paper**: `Zomorodian, A., & Carlsson, G. (2005). Computing Persistent Homology.` [Discrete & Computational Geometry 33(2):249-274]; `Edelsbrunner, H., & Harer, J. (2008). Persistent homology — a survey.` [Contemporary Mathematics, 453, 257-282]; motivation for applying TDA to learned representations: `Naitzat, Zhitnikov & Lim (2020)` [JMLR 21(184):1-40].
* **Paper summary**: Zomorodian & Carlsson give the founding algorithm of computational persistent homology: a filtered simplicial complex decomposes into birth-death intervals (the barcode/persistence diagram), computable by matrix reduction over a field — this is what `ripser` implements. Edelsbrunner & Harer survey the theory. The practical upshot for BLME: lifespans (death − birth) of H0/H1 bars quantify how robustly clusters and loops exist in a representation cloud across all scales at once, with no threshold to tune.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/topology/homology.py` -> `PersistentHomologyTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline keys: `{first,mid,last}_layer_num_loops_h1` and the `_norm` twins `{first,mid,last}_layer_{mean_persistence_h0,max_persistence_h0,mean_persistence_h1}_norm` (lifetime divided by the cloud's mean pairwise distance). Raw lifetimes are in the representation's units and are reported only as `_meta_` (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_topology_homology.py::test_raw_lifetimes_are_meta_only`). The `_norm` twins are declared invariant to isometric re-embedding, zero-padding and rescale (`test_mpd_twins_are_width_and_scale_free`); they still drift with N, so compare models at the same `num_samples`. Per-layer-index keys are `_meta_`, since a fixed index is a different depth in different models. No H1 bar gives a NaN mean H1 lifetime with `num_loops_h1 = 0`. Cost grows quickly with the number of points.

## 2. Persistence Entropy
* **What are we measuring**: The topological disorder and structural noise of the representation manifold.
* **How are we measuring**: Persistent entropy (Atienza et al. 2020, Def. 3.1, log base 2) of the Vietoris-Rips barcode of one point per sequence (the last token of a 20-word window of each passage) at the first, middle and last block, with `num_samples: 1000`. The essential H0 bar is dropped, as giotto-tda's reduced homology does. Each entropy is also reported divided by log2 of the bar count (`_norm`, the maximum-entropy normalisation of Atienza et al. Sec. 3.3).
* **Hypothesis**: High persistence entropy implies the space contains many small, chaotic, short-lived topological artifacts. Low persistence entropy means a few massive, dominant, globally robust semantic features structure the space.
* **Citation/Paper**: `Rucco, M., Castiglione, F., Merelli, E., & Pettini, M. (2016). Characterisation of the Idiotypic Immune Network Through Persistent Entropy.` [Proc. ECCS 2014, Springer]; `Chintakunta, H., Gentimis, T., Gonzalez-Diaz, R., Jimenez, M.-J., & Krim, H. (2015). An entropy-based persistence barcode.` [Pattern Recognition 48(2):391-401]; stability + canonical log2 definition: `Atienza, N., Gonzalez-Diaz, R., & Soriano-Trigueros, M. (2020). On the stability of persistent entropy and new summary functions for topological data analysis.` [Pattern Recognition 107:107509; arXiv:1803.08304].
* **Paper summary**: Rucco et al. introduce persistent entropy — the Shannon entropy of normalized bar lifespans — to give a single scalar for the "disorder" of a barcode, applied there to distinguish dynamical regimes of an immune-network simulation. Chintakunta et al. use the same entropy idea to select significant features within a barcode. Atienza et al. prove stability (small diagram perturbations produce bounded entropy changes) and fix the canonical definition (Def. 3.1, log base 2), which is exactly the formulation BLME implements.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/topology/persistence_entropy.py` -> `PersistenceEntropyTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline keys: `{first,mid,last}_layer_pe_h1` and `{first,mid,last}_layer_pe_h1_norm`, declared invariant to isometric re-embedding, zero-padding and rescale (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_topology_persistence_entropy.py::test_h1_entropy_is_width_and_scale_free`). The H0 family (`pe_h0`, `pe_h0_full`, `pe_h0_norm`, `num_features_h0`) is `_meta_`: the raw H0 entropy of N points is close to log2(N - 1) for any cloud, so it does not separate models. Edge conventions: a single bar or an empty diagram gives 0.0 (giotto-tda fills these differently). Per-layer-index keys are `_meta_`.

## 3. Layer-Wise Topological Complexity (Betti Curves)
* **What are we measuring**: How the shape of language data transforms mathematically from syntax (shallow layers) to abstraction (deep layers).
* **How are we measuring**: Per layer (every block), a symmetric kNN graph is built on one point per sequence (the last token of a 20-word window of each passage; `num_samples: 1000`) after removing exact duplicate points. beta_0 is the number of connected components of the graph. beta_1 is the number of H1 classes alive at a radius of 2 hops on the unit-edge (hop) metric of each component, read with `ripser`. `n_neighbors: auto` uses k = max(14, ceil(log2 N)), counting the point itself.
* **Hypothesis**: Input layers contain highly disconnected, messy word tokens (high Betti curves). Deep conceptual layers merge these into unified structural representations, causing Betti curves to collapse and simplify.
* **Citation/Paper**: `Naitzat, G., Zhitnikov, A., & Lim, L. (2020). Topology of Deep Neural Networks.` [JMLR 21(184):1-40, 2020; arXiv:2004.06093]
* **Paper summary**: Naitzat et al. pass topologically entangled binary datasets through trained ReLU networks and track Betti numbers of each class's point cloud layer by layer, computed on graph-geodesic distances (kNN graph), not raw Euclidean ones. Key findings: well-generalizing networks progressively *simplify* topology — Betti numbers fall with depth until the classes are topologically trivial; the simplification is concentrated in deeper layers; and non-homeomorphic activations (ReLU) enable topology change where smooth activations (tanh) preserve it. BLME computes per-layer β0/β1 curves on LLM hidden states with the same hop-metric construction.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/topology/betti_curve.py` -> `BettiCurveTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline key: `betti_1_curve_mean`, the mean of the beta_1 curve over layers. It is declared invariant to isometric re-embedding, zero-padding and rescale (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_topology_betti_curve.py::test_hop_metric_keys_are_width_and_scale_free`); an inserted identity block reweights the mean, so no identity-block invariance is claimed for it. `h1_max_persistence` is a non-headline feature that is additionally declared invariant to interior and trailing identity blocks. `_meta_max_betti_1`, the beta_0 first/last values and `_meta_simplification_ratio` are `_meta_`. This is an adaptation of Naitzat et al.: the same hop-metric construction, but `ripser` in place of Eirene, no class labels, and k and the radius carried over uncalibrated. The construction is tested on point clouds with known Betti numbers (`tests/tasks/test_topology.py::test_betti_curve_geodesic_recovers_clusters_and_loops`).

## 4. Persistence Landscapes
* **What are we measuring**: Functional summaries of persistence diagrams that retain more structure than scalar lifetime statistics.
* **How are we measuring**: Each birth/death pair becomes a tent function; the k-th landscape is the k-th largest tent value at each t. Landscapes are computed from the Vietoris-Rips barcodes of one point per sequence (the last token of a 20-word window of each passage; `num_samples: 1000`) at the first, middle and last block. Each summary is the mean over `n_subsamples: 16` draws of `subsample_size: 500` points: the mean integral of the first `n_landscapes: 5` landscapes, the maximum of the first landscape, and its L2 norm.
* **Hypothesis**: Larger landscape mass indicates stronger or longer-lived topological features in the sampled representation cloud.
* **Citation/Paper**: `Bubenik, P. (2015). Statistical Topological Data Analysis using Persistence Landscapes.` [JMLR 16:77-102, ArXiv: 1207.6437]. Implementation reference: `Bubenik, P. & Dlotko, P. (2017). A persistence landscapes toolbox for topological statistics.` [ArXiv: 1501.00179]
* **Paper summary**: Bubenik's landscapes map a persistence diagram to a sequence of piecewise-linear functions L_k(t) — the k-th largest tent value at each t — embedding barcodes into a Banach space where means, variances, and hypothesis tests are well-defined (a central limit theorem holds). Landscapes are stable (small diagram changes produce small landscape changes) and, unlike scalar summaries, preserve enough structure to distinguish diagrams with equal total persistence. The toolbox paper supplies the standard discretization algorithms that `persim` (and BLME) follow.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/topology/persistence_landscape.py` -> `PersistenceLandscapeTask`
* **Critical Info**: Requires `ripser`. Headline keys, for each of first/mid/last: `{role}_layer_h1_mean_landscape_integral`, `{role}_layer_h1_landscape_max_1`, and the twins `_mean_landscape_integral_mpd2`, `_landscape_max_1_mpd`, `_landscape_norm_1_mpd15`. The raw summaries carry the representation's units (degree 2, 1 and 1.5); the twins divide by the mean pairwise distance to that power and are the keys declared invariant to isometric re-embedding, zero-padding and rescale (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_topology_persistence_landscape.py::test_mpd_twins_are_width_and_scale_free`). The twins still grow with N, so compare models at the same `num_samples`. H0 summaries are closed forms of the largest H0 deaths and are `_meta_`. Values are checked against `persim` exact landscapes (`tests/tasks/parity/test_persistence_landscape_values.py`).

## 5. Zigzag Persistence (Across-Layer Feature Tracking)
* **What are we measuring**: How individual topological features (loops, components) *persist across the layer axis* — the full evolutionary path of representation topology through the network, rather than one summary per layer aggregated afterwards.
* **How are we measuring**: The protocol of Gardinazzi et al.: one point per prompt (the last token, tokenised without special tokens) at each block's post-attention normalisation and at the final norm, giving L + 1 clouds. Each cloud gets a kNN graph (`n_neighbors: 4`, self excluded, union-symmetrised) filled to its flag complex. The zigzag X_1 <- X_1 n X_2 -> X_2 <- ... is computed with `dionysus` and read in layer units. Descriptors are averaged over `num_subsets: 16` subsets of `subset_size: 500` prompts drawn from a pool of `num_samples: 8000`.
* **Hypothesis**: Coherent semantic structure produces topological features that persist across many consecutive layers (long bands); noise produces short-lived features. Band structure is a model signature that shifts under fine-tuning.
* **Citation/Paper**: `Gardinazzi, Y., Viswanathan, K., Panerai, G., Ansuini, A., Cazzaniga, A., & Biagetti, M. (2025). Persistent Topological Features in Large Language Models.` [ICML 2025; arXiv:2410.11042]. Zigzag persistence theory: `Carlsson, G., & de Silva, V. (2010). Zigzag Persistence.` [Foundations of Computational Mathematics 10:367-405].
* **Paper summary**: Carlsson & de Silva extend persistence to sequences of spaces connected by maps in alternating directions (X₁ → X₁∩X₂ ← X₂ …), which is the structure of "the same prompts, re-embedded at each successive layer". Gardinazzi et al. apply this to LLMs: build a kNN complex per layer plus pairwise intersections, run zigzag homology (dionysus), and introduce descriptors measuring how p-dimensional holes persist and evolve across layers. As a downstream application they derive a layer-pruning criterion from these descriptors.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/topology/zigzag.py` -> `ZigzagPersistenceTask`
* **Critical Info**: Requires `ripser` and `dionysus` (pinned `dionysus==2.1.1`); the task returns an error without it, and there is no fallback construction. Headline keys: `zz_true_adjacent_mean` (mean over adjacent layers of Z_1, the paper's Eq. 7) and `zz_true_mean_bar_len`. The paper's per-layer curves (beta_1, Zbar_1, B_1) are `_meta_` profiles. A ripser-based per-layer loop-band summary and a GF(2) adjacent-layer step are also reported as `_meta_` BLME proxies on the first subset; the band summary does not track feature identity. No key is declared size-invariant: an inserted identity block adds a cloud and moves both headlines (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_topology_zigzag_persistence.py::test_identity_block_insertion_moves_both_headlines`), and the paper compares models of equal depth only. The barcode construction is checked against the reference's effective barcode in `tests/tasks/parity/test_zigzag_reference_construction.py`.
