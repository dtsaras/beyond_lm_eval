# Dynamics Tasks

This module assesses how the model's representations and predictive distribution behave across depth, under perturbation, and along autoregressive generation.

**Registry coverage (10 tasks)**: `dynamics_coe`, `dynamics_context_influence`,
`dynamics_entropy_calibration`, `dynamics_fractal`,
`dynamics_generation_diversity`, `dynamics_gradient_flow`,
`dynamics_interpolation`, `dynamics_perturbation_growth`,
`dynamics_sharpness`, and `dynamics_stability`.

**Paper-faithful vs. BLME adaptation notes**: `dynamics_coe` follows Wang et
al.'s protocol (greedy generation, per-layer mean over the states that emit the
generated tokens). `dynamics_stability` is a seeded embedding-perturbation
adaptation of Wendlandt et al.'s neighbourhood stability, not a
Lyapunov-exponent calculation. `dynamics_perturbation_growth` is a BLME
formulation, not the per-element quasi-Lyapunov protocol.

---

## 1. Embedding-Neighborhood Stability
* **What are we measuring**: How stable token embedding nearest-neighbor neighborhoods are under a reference model comparison or a seeded embedding perturbation.
* **How are we measuring**: Each sampled input-embedding row the tokenizer can emit (ids < len(tokenizer); `num_samples: 5000`) is perturbed by a seeded isotropic direction of 1% of its norm (`noise_std: 0.01`); the cosine `k: 50` nearest-neighbour sets before and after are compared by Jaccard overlap. The perturbation is applied to the whole emittable embedding table and neighbours are searched over all emittable rows; the sampled rows are the queries. With `reference_model_path` set, the second neighbourhood comes from that checkpoint's embeddings instead.
* **Hypothesis**: Robust embedding spaces preserve local neighborhoods under small perturbations or related checkpoints. Low overlap indicates brittle local geometry.
* **Citation/Paper**: BLME diagnostic. Inspiration: `Wendlandt, L., Kummerfeld, J. K., & Mihalcea, R. (2018). Factors Influencing the Surprising Instability of Word Embeddings.` [NAACL-HLT 2018, ArXiv: 1804.09692]. BLME's Jaccard overlap at k = 50 is a related neighbourhood-overlap statistic; it is not a Lyapunov-exponent or Jacobian calculation.
* **Paper summary**: Wendlandt et al. define embedding stability as the percent overlap of the ten nearest neighbors of a word across embedding spaces (different seeds/corpora/algorithms) and show word2vec/GloVe neighborhoods are surprisingly unstable, with frequency and part-of-speech among the drivers — and that downstream models inherit this instability. BLME applies the neighbourhood-overlap idea to LLM input embeddings, under a seeded perturbation of 1% of each row's norm or, with `reference_model_path`, across two checkpoints.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/dynamics/stability.py` -> `NeighborhoodStabilityTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline key: `stability_mean`. The spread (`_meta_stability_std`, `_meta_stability_sem`) is `_meta_`. The only declared invariance is to vocabulary padding: rows past the tokenizer do not enter (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_dynamics_stability.py::test_rows_past_the_tokenizer_do_not_enter`). The value rises under isometric re-embedding into a wider space and depends on how many emittable rows form the neighbourhoods, so no width-free twin is declared. Wendlandt et al. compare independently trained embedding spaces; the seeded perturbation is the BLME substitute for released models with no sibling runs. This is not a Lyapunov exponent.

## 2. Chain of Embedding (CoE)
* **What are we measuring**: How a token representation changes from embedding space through successive transformer layers.
* **How are we measuring**: Greedy generation (`max_new_tokens: 128`) on raw corpus passages without a chat template; per layer, the mean over the generated tokens of the hidden state that emits each of them. The chain runs from the embedding output to the post-final-norm state. Adjacent-layer magnitude and angle changes give Wang et al.'s CoE-R and CoE-C, with the normalised CoE-C form of the released `score.py`. `coe_r_sum` = L x CoE-R is the depth twin.
* **Hypothesis**: Correct and incorrect responses can induce different latent-space trajectories; CoE scores are intended as output-free self-evaluation signals.
* **Citation/Paper**: `Wang, Y., Zhang, P., Yang, B., Wong, D. F., & Wang, R. (2025). Latent Space Chain-of-Embedding Enables Output-free LLM Self-Evaluation.` [ICLR 2025, ArXiv: 2410.13640]
* **Paper summary**: Wang et al. treat the per-layer hidden states of a query (its "chain of embedding") as a latent reasoning trajectory and show its shape differs systematically between correct and incorrect answers, enabling self-evaluation with no output labels. Their scores combine per-step magnitude and angle changes normalized by the end-to-end change (Eq. 2–3), yielding CoE-R (real combination, Eq. 5) and CoE-C (complex-plane centroid magnitude). **Attribution caveat**: the paper's printed Eq. 6/7 uses raw M·exp(iA), its Algorithm 2 normalizes only the magnitude, and the official code (`Alsace08/Chain-of-Embedding score.py`) normalizes both — BLME follows the official code.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/dynamics/coe.py` -> `ChainOfEmbeddingTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline keys: `coe_r`, `coe_c`, `mean_normalized_angle`, `coe_r_std`, `coe_c_std`, `mean_angle_change`, `std_angle_change`, `coe_r_sum`. `coe_r`, `coe_c` and the normalised angle carry the paper's 1/L. Only `coe_r_sum` is declared size-invariant, to identity blocks between existing blocks or before block 0 (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_dynamics_coe.py::test_coe_r_sum_invariant_under_identity_blocks`); a block after the last one is not covered. The paper's printed CoE-C equation uses raw magnitudes while its released code normalises them; BLME follows the released code. The raw magnitude changes are in the representation's units and are `_meta_mean_magnitude_change` / `_meta_std_magnitude_change`. The release reads prompt position 0 for generation step 0; BLME pools the emitting states instead and reports the release's variant as `_meta_coe_*_release_pos0`. The last-prompt-token chain is `_meta_coe_*_last_prompt`.

## 3. Latent Interpolation (Convexity Proxy)
* **What are we measuring**: Whether the latent probability space is continuously convex between two valid representations.
* **How are we measuring**: Each context is cut at `max_tokens: 128` tokens. The post-final-norm last-token states of two passages are interpolated linearly (`steps: 10`, `num_pairs: 200`), each point is rescaled to the linearly interpolated norm, decoded through the model's own LM head, and the entropy of the next-token distribution is read. `convexity_gap` = H(alpha = 0.5) - mean(H(0), H(1)). The forward pass and the decode run in fp32 arithmetic.
* **Hypothesis**: A robust representation space should generally construct smooth gradients between concepts. If the exact middle point ($0.5 h_1 + 0.5 h_2$) collapses into extreme entropy (random noise), the semantic space is severely non-convex and structurally brittle.
* **Citation/Paper**: BLME diagnostic; the slerp comparison follows `White, T. (2016). Sampling Generative Networks.` [ArXiv: 1609.04468] (which credits Shoemake 1985), via the official `dribnet/plat` implementation.
* **Paper summary**: White shows that linear interpolation between Gaussian latent points leaves the high-probability shell of the prior, and proposes spherical linear interpolation (slerp, Shoemake's great-arc formula) so interpolation paths stay on-manifold — the standard trick for latent-space traversals in generative models. BLME interpolates linearly between final hidden states with a norm correction, decodes through the LM head, and reads the next-token entropy; slerp is kept as a `_meta_` comparison. The convexity gap is BLME's own diagnostic.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/dynamics/trajectories.py` -> `LatentInterpolationTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline key: `convexity_gap`, declared invariant to identity blocks at interior, leading and trailing placements (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_dynamics_interpolation.py::test_convexity_gap_invariant_under_identity_blocks`). The norm correction avoids a mechanical midpoint norm collapse that would raise entropy for geometric reasons alone. With the same norm target, slerp coincides with the linear path at alpha in {0, 0.5, 1}, so it is kept only as a `_meta_` comparison. The endpoint entropies are the model's own predictive entropy, so the gap contains a capability term; it is disclosed in `_meta_convexity_gap_endpoint_entropy_mean`. The result depends on the unembedding matrix as well as on the transformer blocks.

## 4. Loss-Landscape Sharpness
* **What are we measuring**: Local curvature of the model's loss around the current parameters.
* **How are we measuring**: On a configurable parameter scope (`param_scope: last_layer`) and in fp32 arithmetic: PyHessian's loops (one Hessian-vector product per iteration, tolerance 1e-3, at most 100 iterations) for the Hutchinson trace and the signed top eigenvalue; the SAM loss increase at `sam_rho: 0.05`; its adaptive, scale-invariant form `asam_sharpness` (Kwon et al. 2021); and the critical step size, found by probing theta - eta x g along the raw gradient (doubling, then bisection) for the largest eta* with non-increasing loss, with `critical_sharpness` = 2 / eta* (Kalra et al. 2026, arXiv:2601.16979, who probe the optimizer's update direction instead).
* **Hypothesis**: Sharper local curvature can indicate brittle optimization geometry or poorer generalization.
* **Citation/Paper**: `Foret, P., Kleiner, A., Mobahi, H., & Neyshabur, B. (2021). Sharpness-Aware Minimization for Efficiently Improving Generalization.` [ICLR 2021, ArXiv: 2010.01412]; `Yao, Z., Gholami, A., Keutzer, K., & Mahoney, M. (2020). PyHessian: Neural Networks Through the Lens of the Hessian.` [IEEE Big Data 2020, ArXiv: 1912.07145]; Hutchinson trace: `Hutchinson (1990)`; `Bai, Fahey & Golub (1996)`.
* **Paper summary**: Foret et al. link the sharpness of the loss landscape to generalization and train by minimizing the worst-case loss in a ρ-ball; their Eq. 2 (p=2) perturbation ε̂ = ρ·∇L/‖∇L‖ is exactly BLME's SAM-sharpness probe L(θ+ε̂)−L(θ). Yao et al.'s PyHessian makes Hessian spectral analysis tractable for deep nets via Hessian-vector products: top eigenvalues by power iteration (signed Rayleigh quotient), trace by Hutchinson's stochastic estimator, and shows these track training phenomena (batch size, residual connections). BLME implements both probes on the last-block parameter subset by default so the cost stays bounded for large models.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/dynamics/sharpness.py` -> `LossSharpnessTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline keys: `baseline_loss`, `hutchinson_trace_estimate`, `hutchinson_trace_per_param`, `top_eigenvalue_estimate`, `sam_sharpness`, `sam_perturbed_loss`, `critical_step_size`, `critical_sharpness`, `asam_sharpness`. Only `asam_sharpness` is declared size-invariant, to a function-preserving residual rescale (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_dynamics_sharpness.py::test_asam_is_invariant_under_a_function_preserving_rescale`); the trace, the top eigenvalue, plain SAM and the critical step move under it. Second-order derivatives may require eager attention instead of SDPA/Flash Attention; the task detects this and returns an error. Probe vectors are seeded (`seed`, default 42). The critical-step search returns NaN when the gradient is zero or when no stable step is found. `critical_sharpness` is a directional curvature along the gradient, not the top Hessian eigenvalue. The batch is the first `num_samples` passages, each cut at 64 tokens; the loss is the mean of the per-passage mean cross-entropies, and every probe (Hessian, SAM, ASAM, critical step) uses that one objective. `asam_variant` chooses the ASAM normaliser: `invariant` (default, T_w = |w| on every parameter, exactly scale-invariant) or `release` (asam.py: |w| + `asam_eta` on tensors named `weight`, 1 elsewhere; 2.7x larger on gpt2); the other is recorded as `_meta_asam_sharpness_<other>`. `critical_step_size` is resolved to ±0.8%: 6 bisection steps halve a factor-2 bracket, and the bracket midpoint is returned.

## 5. Gradient Flow
* **What are we measuring**: Per-layer gradient norm propagation under next-token cross-entropy.
* **How are we measuring**: Hooks capture every block input and the final residual (the input of the final norm). The model's own next-token cross-entropy on held-out text (the first 64 tokens of each document) is backpropagated in fp32, and the Frobenius norm of the gradient at each site is taken over every position except the sink rows, then averaged over documents. The headline is the end-to-end gain log(‖g_emb‖ / ‖g_final‖), where g_emb is the gradient at the first block's input (token plus position embedding on GPT-2).
* **Hypothesis**: Vanishing or exploding gradients across depth indicate training or conditioning pathologies.
* **Citation/Paper**: `Pascanu, R., Mikolov, T., & Bengio, Y. (2013). On the difficulty of training recurrent neural networks.` [ICML 2013, ArXiv: 1211.5063]
* **Paper summary**: Pascanu et al. formalize vanishing/exploding gradients in recurrent nets: the error signal decomposes into products of step-to-step Jacobians (their Sec. 1.1, Eq. 3–5), whose norms shrink or blow up exponentially (Sec. 2), and they propose clipping and a regularizer. BLME maps the time axis to the depth axis of a transformer: ‖dL/dh_l‖_F at each block input plays the role of the per-step gradient norm. The end-to-end log ratio and the `_meta_` profile summaries are BLME's own.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/dynamics/gradient_flow.py` -> `GradientFlowTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline key: `gradient_flow_log_ratio_in_out`, declared invariant to identity blocks at interior, leading and trailing placements and to a common residual rescale (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_dynamics_gradient_flow.py::test_end_to_end_log_ratio_invariant_under_identity_blocks`). Entropy, slope, vanishing ratio and the raw norms move under one of those transformations and are `_meta_`. This is a diagnostic backward pass on a frozen model, not a training run; activation gradients are used because they are defined for a frozen model and do not depend on block width or parameterisation. Pascanu et al. study gradients through time in RNNs; BLME maps time to depth.

## 6. Generation Diversity
* **What are we measuring**: Diversity and repetition collapse in sampled continuations.
* The default run samples 500 completions with temperature 0.8, at most 32 new tokens and top-k disabled. It computes Distinct-n and Self-BLEU over NLTK words (orders 2–5, with order 4 as `mean_self_bleu`) and the repeated 4-gram rate. For each generation step it normalizes the processed logits in float64 and sums Shannon entropy in nats, assigning zero contribution to masked tokens. Completions with at least four content tokens contribute equally to the first- and last-quarter means and their difference.
* **Hypothesis**: Degenerate generation often shows low distinct-n, high Self-BLEU, falling entropy, and repeated phrases.
* **Citation/Paper**: `Li, J., Galley, M., Brockett, C., Gao, J., & Dolan, B. (2016). A Diversity-Promoting Objective Function for Neural Conversation Models.` [NAACL 2016, ArXiv: 1510.03055]; `Zhu, Y., Lu, S., Zheng, L., Guo, J., Zhang, W., Wang, J., & Yu, Y. (2018). Texygen: A Benchmarking Platform for Text Generation Models.` [SIGIR 2018, ArXiv: 1802.01886]
* **Paper summary**: Li et al. observe that neural conversation models collapse onto bland high-frequency replies and propose a mutual-information objective; their distinct-1/2 metrics (distinct n-grams scaled by total generated tokens) became the standard diversity readout. Zhu et al.'s Texygen benchmarking platform contributes Self-BLEU — each generated text BLEU-scored against the other generations — where high Self-BLEU means mode collapse. BLME reports distinct-n over nltk words, per completion and over the collection, and the Texygen Self-BLEU procedure.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/dynamics/generation_diversity.py` -> `GenerationDiversityTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline keys: `distinct_1..3`, `mean_distinct_1..3`, `mean_self_bleu`, `mean_phrase_repetition_rate`, `mean_sampling_entropy_first_quarter`, `mean_sampling_entropy_last_quarter`, `sampling_entropy_collapse_delta`. The word-unit keys (Self-BLEU and the distinct-n family) are declared independent of the tokenizer for a fixed text (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_dynamics_generation_diversity.py::test_word_unit_metrics_do_not_depend_on_the_tokenizer`); the phrase-repetition and sampling-entropy keys have no invariance declaration. Corpus distinct-n depends on the collection size, which is pinned at 500 completions for every model. Sampling is seeded through the `seed` config (default 42 in `defaults.yaml`). The completions come from 8 bundled prompts. Distinct-n and Self-BLEU count `nltk.word_tokenize` words (Texygen's unit) with a total-token denominator; phrase repetition counts subword tokens. When a completion ends at EOS, its first and last entropy quarters can differ in length.

## 7. Fractal Structure of the Likelihood Stream (dynamics_fractal)
* **What are we measuring**: Self-similarity and long-range dependence of the model's next-token log-loss viewed as a stochastic process — the "fractal signature" of language as seen by the model.
* **How are we measuring**: Whole WikiText-103 articles of at least 4096 words (`min_doc_words: 4096`, selected by word count so every tokenizer gets the same articles; `num_samples: 100`). The first min(`use_tokens: 1024`, model context) tokens of each article are scored in a single forward pass and a 128-token burn-in prefix is dropped. z_t = -log p(x_t | prefix) is standardised per document and integrated to X_t. The Hurst exponent comes from rescaled-range analysis over log-spaced windows, the Joseph exponent J from sigma_tau ~ tau^J, and the self-similarity (Hölder) exponent S from p_eps(tau) ~ tau^-S (`holder_eps: 0.005`). The three parameters are fitted on the pooled per-document statistics.
* **Hypothesis**: Language's loss stream is self-similar with long-range dependence (H ≈ 0.6–0.75, above the 0.5 of memoryless noise); Alabdulmohsin et al. report the Hurst/Joseph structure is robust across domains and that fractal parameters relate to downstream performance beyond raw loss.
* **Citation/Paper**: `Alabdulmohsin, I., Tran, V. Q., & Dehghani, M. (2024). Fractal Patterns May Illuminate the Success of Next-Token Prediction.` [NeurIPS 2024, ArXiv: 2402.01825]
* **Paper summary**: The paper formalizes the intuition that language is self-similar and long-range dependent by studying the bits-per-token stream of an LM as a stochastic process: they estimate Hölder (self-similarity) and Joseph/Hurst (long-range dependence) exponents, find language sits robustly at H ≈ 0.70 ± 0.09 — poised between predictability and noise, unlike white noise (0.5) or heavily-persistent processes — and show the exponents are stable across granularities, domains and model families, arguing this partially explains why next-token prediction transfers to long-horizon competence. A joint fractal parameter marginally improves downstream-performance prediction over perplexity alone.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/dynamics/fractal.py` -> `FractalLikelihoodTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline keys: `hurst_exponent`, `joseph_exponent`, `self_similarity_exponent`, their per-document means (`*_perdoc`) and standard deviations (`*_std`). No key is declared size-invariant: the exponents are dimensionless but track the model's NLL process. Every model scores the same number of tokens per article unless its context is shorter than `use_tokens`. Concatenated-stream values are reported as `_meta_*_concat_legacy`. The self-similarity exponent is weakly identified at small budgets and is sensitive to eps, which is recorded as `_meta_holder_eps`. Because each document is standardised before pooling, white noise gives J of about 0.44 at the shipped lag range instead of 0.5. The estimators are checked in `tests/tasks/parity/test_fractal_pooled_parity.py`. Lag grids: the Joseph and self-similarity exponents use 10 log-spaced integer lags (floored, deduplicated) from τ_min = min(25, n_min/8) to n_min/4; the Hurst exponent uses 12 log-spaced windows from max(8, min(25, n_min/8)) to n_min/2, where n_min is the shortest stream. The self-similarity exponent is weakly identified (per-document SD 0.57 on gpt2).

## 8. Layer Perturbation Growth (dynamics_perturbation_growth)
* **What are we measuring**: Whether small input perturbations amplify or contract as they propagate through depth — a chaoticity / sensitivity profile of the forward dynamics.
* **How are we measuring**: A seeded isotropic perturbation of norm `rel_eps: 0.01` x ‖e_t‖ is added to every input embedding; clean and perturbed forward passes run in fp32 arithmetic. Per layer, r_l is the token mean (sink rows excluded) of ‖h_l - h'_l‖ / ‖h_l‖, over the first 128 tokens of each document. The headline is the end-to-end relative amplification `growth_log_ratio_relative` = log(r_L / r_0). r_0 is read at the first hidden state, so on GPT-2 (learned position embeddings added after the token embedding) its relative perturbation is about 0.0066 rather than 0.01; on rotary models it is 0.01.
* **Hypothesis**: Positive growth slopes indicate a chaotic amplifying regime, which Li et al. associate with cognitive/reasoning activation in LLMs; contraction indicates dissipative, stable forward dynamics. The depth profile localizes where sensitivity concentrates.
* **Citation/Paper**: grounding — `Li, X., et al. (2025). Cognitive Activation and Chaotic Dynamics in Large Language Models: A Quasi-Lyapunov Analysis of Reasoning Mechanisms.` [ArXiv: 2503.13530]; residual-stream dynamics context: Fernando & Guitchounts (ArXiv: 2502.12131, 2605.14258).
* **Paper summary**: Li et al. treat the layer stack as a dynamical system and measure a quasi-Lyapunov exponent — the growth rate of hidden-state divergence across layers under small input perturbations — finding that LLMs operate in a weakly-chaotic regime where information gets selectively amplified, and that amplification correlates with reasoning engagement ("cognitive activation"). The residual-stream dynamics line (Fernando & Guitchounts) shows layer-Jacobian spectra govern whether perturbations grow or decay through depth.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/dynamics/perturbation_growth.py` -> `PerturbationGrowthTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline key: `growth_log_ratio_relative`, declared invariant to identity blocks at interior, leading and trailing placements (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_dynamics_perturbation_growth.py::test_growth_log_ratio_invariant_under_identity_blocks`). The absolute log(d_L / d_0) mostly tracks residual-norm growth and is `_meta_`, as are the per-block rate and the normalised-depth slope. This is a BLME formulation, not Li et al.'s quasi-Lyapunov exponent: their exponent perturbs one element of one intermediate-layer row and tracks per-position divergence, too costly to run on every model, and their Eq. 9 has no state-norm normalisation and a 1/(n - m) factor. `_meta_growth_rate_per_block` is the closest analogue. Requires `inputs_embeds` support.

## 9. Entropy Calibration Error (dynamics_entropy_calibration)
* **What are we measuring**: Whether the model's generations stay as uncertain as its fit to human text warrants: its entropy along its own samples against its log loss on the human continuation.
* **How are we measuring**: The released protocol on WikiText-103 train articles: the first 128 tokens are the prompt, the model samples up to 1024 tokens at temperature 1 (pure sampling), and EntCE = mean per-step entropy along the samples - mean log loss along the human continuation, pooled over all steps (nats per token). `entropy_calibration_ratio` = H / L - 1 removes each tokenizer's per-token units to first order.
* **Hypothesis**: Cao et al. find that EntCE shrinks only slowly with model size (scaling exponent near 0 on text) and that newer families (Llama 3, Qwen2.5) are better calibrated than older ones (Llama 2, Pythia): a lineage effect.
* **Citation/Paper**: `Cao, S., et al. (2025). On the Entropy Calibration of Language Models.` [NeurIPS 2025, ArXiv: 2511.11966]; code stevenxcao/entropy-calibration.
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/dynamics/entropy_calibration.py` -> `EntropyCalibrationTask`
* **Critical Info**: Headline keys: `entropy_calibration_error` and `entropy_calibration_ratio`, both declared invariant to identity blocks at interior, leading and trailing placements (`tests/tasks/invariance/test_dynamics_entropy_calibration.py::test_headline_invariant`). Cost: `num_samples: 500` articles x 1024 sampled tokens plus two scoring passes per article, so run it on a GPU. Departures from the release: HF sampling instead of vLLM; 1024-token samples (the paper's limit; the code allows 2048); 500 articles instead of 5000, with `_meta_entce_ci95` from a bootstrap over articles; articles kept by byte length so every model scores the same ones; scoring with the model as loaded (the release rescores with a 4-bit NF4 copy); and the log loss aligned to the response tokens (the release reads one position late). Sampling is seeded inside a forked RNG, so the task does not move the global RNG.

## 10. Context influence (dynamics_context_influence)
* **What are we measuring**: How strongly the next-token distribution depends on a single token k positions back, as a function of k.
* **How are we measuring**: In 1024-token windows (every model; GPT-2's context) cut at a seeded word offset from 96 WikiText-103 train articles of at least 2048 words, three positions s in [16, 64) per window are replaced in turn by a token drawn from the windows' unigram distribution, and the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the clean and perturbed next-token distributions is taken at every later position (lag k = t - s + 1, up to 961, the largest lag every replacement observes). I(k) is the mean over windows and replacements, in nats (at most log 2). `influence_decay_exponent` is beta of I(k) ~ k^-beta fitted in log-log over log2 lag bins from lag 3; `influence_far_mass` is sum_{k>=9} I(k); `influence_horizon_bytes` is the influence-weighted geometric mean lag times the windows' mean bytes per token. Forwards run in fp32 arithmetic at every size; divergences in float64.
* **Hypothesis**: The memo of 2026-11-21 (section 4.3) found a power-law decay with a family-specific exponent (GPT-2 1.24-1.30, Pythia 1.11-1.16 at 256-token windows), an influence of at most 3e-4 beyond lag 1 at random initialisation, and more far mass in Pythia than GPT-2 at equal width and loss: a candidate for signal beyond loss.
* **Citation/Paper**: BLME construct after `Khandelwal, U., et al. (2018). Sharp Nearby, Fuzzy Far Away: How Neural Language Models Use Context.` [ACL 2018, ArXiv: 1805.04623]; `Lin, H. W., & Tegmark, M. (2017). Critical Behavior in Physics and Probabilistic Formal Languages.` [ArXiv: 1606.06737]; `Cifka, O., & Liutkus, A. (2023). Black-box language model explanation by context length probing.` [ArXiv: 2212.14815].
* **File & Function**: `src/blme/tasks/dynamics/context_influence.py` -> `ContextInfluenceTask`
* **Critical Info**: Exploratory, not part of the paper core. bf16 forwards are not usable: on gpt2 and pythia-160m the long-lag bins sit at a rounding floor (0.005 and 0.15 nats per lag) and the far mass rises 10x and 230x, so `compute_dtype: float32` is the default and is recorded (`_meta_compute_dtype`). Position 0 and massive-activation tokens are never replaced and their predictions are not scored. Every random stream (window offsets, replaced positions, replacement tokens, bootstrap) derives from `seed` (42) and the window index; `_meta_draw_digest` fingerprints the draws. `_meta_pre_replacement_max_js` must be 0 (a prediction before the replaced position cannot move). The per-lag profile, the binned profile with its lag bins in tokens and bytes, and window-bootstrap SEs are `_meta_`. Lags are tokenizer units; read the horizon in bytes across tokenizers. A model with a context under 1024 is measured on a shorter window and flagged (`_meta_window_is_configured`).
