# Evaluation metrics

Generated by `scripts/metrics_doc.py` from the task registry, `task_metadata.py`, the shipped defaults, `docs/validation/metrics.json`, `docs/validation/independent_reviews.yaml` and `docs/validation/decisions.yaml`. Edit those, not this file.

**109 registered tasks.** The 51 paper-core tasks and the companion study's 2 covariates have full entries below; the others are listed at the end.

Each entry gives what the task measures, its headline keys, its references, how it was verified, its parameters with their shipped defaults, and any design decision. Two kinds of verification are listed:

- **Kernel**: the core computation against a reference on identical inputs (from `docs/validation/metrics.json`).
- **Pipeline**: an independent re-implementation of the whole path from the model's forward pass, by a reviewer who did not read the task's code (from `docs/validation/independent_reviews.yaml`).

## Conventions shared by many tasks

- **Sink rows in token clouds and pools.** Drop position 0 and every token whose hidden-state norm exceeds 10x its document's median token norm at any interior depth (TOKEN_POLICY exclude_sinks). Massive-activation tokens carry most of the centred variance (gpt2 L6: 1% of rows hold 96% of it) and are a positional artefact, not content; non-LLM-origin metrics would otherwise measure them.
- **One point per sequence.** The last token of a 20-word window cut from each passage at a hashed offset; the same words for every model (points_readout: window). Whole passages are opt-in (points_readout: passage). Identical text across tokenizers; the last-token state is the one whose next-token distribution is defined.
- **Massive activations at the last token of a window.** Kept (massive_points: keep), as the references keep every point; every one-point-per-sequence cloud records _meta_window_n_massive_points and _meta_window_massive_points_max_ratio. massive_points: drop removes those windows at every depth, using the token-cloud sink rule (norm > 10x the median last-token norm at a middle depth). Rare but not absent: 1 of 979 windows on OLMo-1B (a window ending in '.', 54x the median) and on pythia-160m, 28 on SmolLM2-1.7B, 0 on gpt2, Llama-3.2, gemma-2/3/4, phi-2, Qwen2.5/3. One such point sets a cloud's extremal statistics: OLMo's mid_layer_max_persistence_h0_norm is 22.7 against ~1 on the other models.
- **Which state 'the last layer' is.** Tasks differ, and each records _meta_last_entry_is_block_output. HF's post-final-norm hidden_states[-1]: geometry_svd, the matrix_entropy headline, isoscore, magnitude, phd_dimension and cknna's last entry. The raw deepest block output: lid and intrinsic_dim (layerwise), vendi, cka, hsic, rsa and the per-layer matrix-entropy profile. The final norm changes values a lot (gpt2 effective rank 237 post-norm vs 596 pre-norm; Llama IsoScore 0.053 vs 0.0006), so the same key name on two tasks is not the same representation. Each task follows its reference's readout where one exists.
- **Attention kernel.** Eager whenever a co-run task needs attention weights (the study's full runs always are); recorded in the envelope as config.attn_implementation. bf16 eager and sdpa give different logits (gemma-4-E2B mean NLL 2.2434 vs 2.2407); fp32 is identical across kernels and transformers versions.

When several settings are defensible and the evidence does not pick one, the choice is a parameter whose default is the paper's or released code's protocol; where the alternative is cheap, every run also records it as a `_meta_` key, so both can be compared without rerunning.

## Geometry

### `geometry_categories`

Semantic-category geometry of the input embedding table (BLME construct).

**Protocol.** The task asks whether semantically related words sit together in the input embedding table: per category of single-token words it reports cosine distances within the category and to 100 random emittable tokens, k-NN purity over the emittable vocabulary with k capped at m-1, hubness inside the category, and for relation pairs the consistency of offset vectors. Alternatives considered: probing contextual hidden states (needs a forward pass and a context per word) and the causal-inner-product polytopes of Park et al. 2024, which are better grounded but need WordNet hierarchies and the unembedding covariance. The input table was chosen because it is weights-only and cheap. Its limit is that the word-to-token mapping is tokenizer-specific (only single-token words count), so values are not cross-model comparable; every draw and search uses only rows the tokenizer can emit, so unused rows cannot move a value.

**Headline keys.** `{category}_intra`, `{category}_inter`, `{category}_separation`, `{category}_purity`, `{category}_hub_skew`, `{category}_hub_max`, `{relation}_consistency`.

**References.** paper: BLME category geometry; code: `none`; LLM precedent: arXiv:2406.01506 (Park et al. 2024, categorical concepts in LLM representation space; alternative considered).

**Tokens.** `not_token_based`.

**Verification.**

- Kernel: BLME proxy: an operational definition of its own. Within-category distances and retrieval statistics use curated single-token lists. Tokenization and the choice of categories govern what generalizes.
- Pipeline: match except inter (fixed baseline draw) (batchB, 2026-11-19, library fbce8b8).
- Next scientific check: Repeat with held-out categories and multi-token forms across tokenizers.

**Parameters (shipped defaults).** `categories_path: null`, `k_purity: 20`, `projection_method: null`.

**Decision: Random-token baseline for inter-category similarity.** Default: A fixed draw of 100 tokens, independent of the run seed.
Why: Exploratory task; the draw sits in the upper tail of re-draws (gpt2 one-sided p = 0.018), shifting inter by +0.002-0.012.

### `geometry_collapse`

Detects representation collapse by tracking Effective Rank across layers. A sharp drop in effective rank indicates dimensional collapse.

**Protocol.** At each block the Roy-Vetterli effective rank of one centred cloud pooling every token of every document is taken, following the covariance construction of Jing et al.; collapse_ratio (min/max over blocks) summarises the profile. Queipo-de-Llano et al. instead compute, per sequence and without centring, the entropy of s_j^2/||X||_F^2 with the BOS/first-token row kept, and that row produces the compression valley; qdl_entropy_min reports that valley as the paper does, on the first 64 tokens of every passage that has them: at the valley the entropy grows with the number of rows, so whole passages would add a tokenizer term (the whole-passage value is _meta_). In the pooled cloud the same row carries most of the centred variance at middle blocks, so the Roy-Vetterli keys drop position 0 and massive-norm rows (Sun et al. 2024) with one mask for all layers, and then measure non-sink dimensional spread (gpt2 collapse_ratio 0.398 -> 0.825). On 28 bank models they still separate models (between/within-model SD ratio 40 for collapse_ratio, 131 for min_erank), so they stay headline. max_drop and the per-layer lists depend on how the network is split into blocks and are _meta_. The pooled erank grows with the token count up to min(N-1, d), so models are compared at one document count.

**Headline keys.** `qdl_entropy_min`, `max_erank`, `min_erank`, `collapse_ratio`.

**References.** paper: Jing et al. 2021 (ICLR 2022); Roy & Vetterli 2007; Queipo-de-Llano et al. 2025 (arXiv:2510.06477); code: `none released for arXiv:2510.06477 (checked 2026-10-28)`; LLM precedent: arXiv:2510.06477 (per-sequence matrix entropy at every layer, the compression valley); arXiv:2502.02013 (Skean et al. 2025, prompt entropy per layer).

**Tokens.** `exclude_sinks`.

**Verification.**

- Kernel: Equations and analytic cases (no external reference exists). Roy–Vetterli effective rank uses normalized singular values. Loss of numerical rank does not identify its semantic cause.
- Pipeline: match (batchA, 2026-11-19, library fbce8b8).
- Next scientific check: Test whether induced rank loss changes a separately measured behavioural outcome.

**Parameters (shipped defaults).** `num_samples: 100`, `qdl_layers: all`.

**Decision: Blocks over which the Queipo-de-Llano valley is read (parameter `qdl_layers`).** Default: all: the minimum of the per-layer profile over every block.
Alternatives: interior: blocks 1..L-2.
Recorded in every run: `_meta_qdl_entropy_min_interior`, `_meta_qdl_entropy_min_interior_layer_frac`, `_meta_qdl_layers`.
Why: The paper's compression valley is mid-depth; pythia-160m's all-block minimum is the final pre-norm block (0.266) while its mid-depth valley is 0.80, whereas gpt2's minimum is mid-depth.

### `geometry_correlation_dimension`

Computes the Grassberger-Procaccia fractional correlation dimension on the final representation space to evaluate fractal complexity: one point per document (the last token of a 20-word window), Du & Tanaka-Ishii 2025's fixed fit window.

**Protocol.** The correlation dimension D2 (Grassberger & Procaccia 1983) is the small-radius slope of log C(r), where C(r) is the fraction of point pairs closer than r. Its LLM applications (Du & Tanaka-Ishii 2024/2025) measure the trajectory of next-token probability vectors along one long text; BLME instead measures how the model spreads documents, with one point per document (the last token of a 20-word window of each of 1000 passages at the output representation, Euclidean distance), the per-sequence convention of the LLM intrinsic-dimension literature. The fit uses Du & Tanaka-Ishii's published window, C in [20/(N(N-1)), 1/N], a function of N alone, so the value moves continuously with the data; the plateau search it replaces jumped under 1e-3 perturbations. D2 at fixed N converges from below and exceeds the 2 log10 N resolution bound for high-dimensional clouds, so N is pinned for every model. The mean-pooled document cloud (position 0 and massive rows excluded) is kept as _meta_.

**Headline keys.** `correlation_dimension`.

**References.** paper: Grassberger & Procaccia 1983 (PRL 50(5):346-349); Du & Tanaka-Ishii 2025 (fixed C window); code: `CSchoel/nolds@f121feebf6b2cbd67a8cb855bd0fe8c39add3ad9`; LLM precedent: Du & Tanaka-Ishii 2025 arXiv:2510.21258 (correlation dimension of LLM next-token log-probability vectors; fit window App. A).

**Tokens.** `last_per_sequence`.

**Verification.**

- Kernel: Equations and analytic cases (no external reference exists). Known-dimensional clouds and an independently computed correlation integral anchor the log-log slope. The fixed fitting window, sample size and density can change the estimated dimension.
- Pipeline: match (batchA, 2026-11-19, library fbce8b8).
- Next scientific check: Vary the fit interval, sample count and density on known-dimensional clouds.

**Parameters (shipped defaults).** `max_points: 2000`, `num_radii: 30`, `num_samples: 1000`, `token_cloud_docs: 200`.

**Decision: Where the correlation integral is evaluated.** Default: At the sorted pair distances with C(d_(k)) = k/M, OLS of log C on log r in Du & Tanaka-Ishii's window 20/(N(N-1)) <= C <= 1/N.
Alternatives: a radius grid or midpoints (+1-2.5%); strict < (+2.5%).
Recorded in every run: `_meta_correlation_dimension_under_resolved`.
Why: The papers do not fix it; estimates exceed the Eckmann-Ruelle bound 2 log10 N at N <= 1000 and are flagged under-resolved.

### `geometry_hubness`

Analyzes the "hubness" of the embedding space: the skewness of the distribution of k-nearest neighbor occurrences.

**Protocol.** Hubness is the skewness S_Nk of the k-occurrence distribution (Radovanovic et al. 2010): how unevenly points appear in each other's k-nearest-neighbour lists. BLME measures it on the unembedding rows, as Nielsen et al. (2025) do for LLM vocabularies, with cosine distance (unit rows, chord ranking), at the paper's k=10 plus k=50 and k=100; the input-embedding table is _meta_. The Gini coefficient and the top-1% mass of N_k are BLME additions: bounded inequality summaries that are less driven by a single hub than the skew. Only rows the tokenizer can emit are ranked, rows without a direction are left out, and a seeded draw of 32000 rows (the smallest study vocabulary) is used because S_Nk grows with the number of rows.

**Headline keys.** `hubness_k10_skew`, `hubness_k10_max`, `hubness_k10_top1pct`, `hubness_k10_gini`, `hubness_k50_skew`, `hubness_k50_max`, `hubness_k50_top1pct`, `hubness_k50_gini`, `hubness_k100_skew`, `hubness_k100_max`, `hubness_k100_top1pct`, `hubness_k100_gini`.

**References.** paper: Radovanovic et al. 2010 (JMLR 11); Tomasev et al. 2014 (IEEE TKDE 26(3)); Nielsen et al. 2025 (LLM vocabularies); code: `VarIr/scikit-hubness@c36a058e67c34696182d3b8c5da089e287eb60bf`; LLM precedent: Nielsen, Macocco, Baroni 2025 arXiv:2502.10201 (k-occurrence hubness of the unembedding rows, k=10).

**Tokens.** `not_token_based`.

**Verification.**

- Kernel: Independent implementation or scientific library. Self-free k-NN graph agrees with scikit-hubness. The library’s default self-query includes its own neighbour, which changes the population. Width, k and duplicate handling matter.
- Pipeline: match (<= 7e-5); Llama-3 reserved rows are ranked (now a parameter) (batchC, 2026-11-19, library 1ae4105).
- Next scientific check: Vary k and duplicate frequency with self-neighbours explicitly controlled.

**Parameters (shipped defaults).** `batch_size: 2000`, `drop_near_duplicates: False`, `near_duplicate_gap: 0.0001`.

**Decision: Near-duplicate rows of the 32000-row draw (parameter `drop_near_duplicates`).** Default: false: ranked like every other emittable row.
Alternatives: true: rows within near_duplicate_gap (1 - cos, default 1e-4) of another drawn row are dropped.
Recorded in every run: `_meta_n_near_duplicate_rows`, `_meta_without_near_duplicates_<key> (or _meta_with_near_duplicates_<key>)`.
Why: The emittable-row rule (id < len(tokenizer)) keeps untrained in-vocabulary rows: Llama-3's reserved tokens (290 rows within 1e-6 of another) and Qwen3's 1961 bitwise-identical never-emitted byte tokens. Dropping Llama's from the draw moves hubness_k50_skew 19.10 -> 26.19. But the nearest-neighbour gap is a continuum on other models (gemma-3: 3498 rows under 1e-4, 5144 under 1e-3) with no natural cut, so it is a parameter; the alternative costs a second ranking pass only on models that have near-duplicates.

### `geometry_intrinsic_dim`

Estimates the Intrinsic Dimension (ID) of the embedding manifold using the Two-NN estimator.

**Protocol.** TwoNN (Facco et al. 2017) estimates intrinsic dimension from the ratio of each point's second- to first-nearest-neighbour distance, fitted through the origin with the top 10% of ratios discarded. In layerwise mode BLME applies it to one point per sequence at every block output: the last token of a 20-word window of each of 1000 passages (the same words and N for every model), Euclidean distance, as Cheng et al. 2024 do with the last token. A token cloud pooled across documents is avoided because 28-83% of a token's nearest neighbours lie in its own document; its value is kept as _meta_tokens_* with position 0 and massive rows excluded. TwoNN falls as N grows (Facco et al. report d against N), so the value is the ID at a fixed 1000-point scale. The static mode measures the input-embedding rows, whose count is the vocabulary size.

**Headline keys.** `lid_first_layer`, `lid_mid_layer`, `lid_last_layer`.

**References.** paper: Facco et al. 2017 (Sci Rep 7:12140); Ansuini et al. 2019; code: `AnsuiniAlessio/IntrinsicDimDeep@2c5140009eb3`; LLM precedent: Cheng et al. 2024 arXiv:2405.15471 (last token of each sequence, every layer); Valeriani et al. 2023 arXiv:2302.00294 (one point per sequence, TwoNN per layer).

**Tokens.** `last_per_sequence`.

**Verification.**

- Kernel: Released dataset and a published numerical value. The static estimator recovers the released 2,500-point Swiss roll within the published-data tolerance. Across five article-disjoint 200-passage validation panels on GPT-2, Pythia-160M and SmolLM2-1.7B, first-layer variation was 32–36% of each model’s mean. On five independent training-split panels with one passage per article, increasing the budget from 200 to 1,000 articles reduced first-layer variation from 17–29% to 3.2–7.3%. Some model rankings still changed between draws.
- Pipeline: match (<= 3e-8 vs DADApy compute_id_2NN); its _meta_ token cloud exceeded task_timeout on CPU (fixed: token_cloud auto) (batchC, 2026-11-19, library 1ae4105).
- Next scientific check: Report sampling uncertainty when comparing close checkpoints. Generalization of the 1,000-article stability result beyond these three checkpoints is untested.

**Parameters (shipped defaults).** `layerwise: True`, `num_samples: 1000`, `token_cloud: auto`, `token_cloud_docs: 200`.

**Decision: Static (vocabulary embedding) or layerwise (per-layer point clouds) mode (parameter `layerwise`).** Default: true: TwoNN per layer on one point per sequence (lid_first/mid/last_layer).
Alternatives: false: the static vocabulary-embedding estimate (intrinsic_dimension).
Why: The headline keys exist only in layerwise mode, which is what the study runs; the static mode has no headline key and takes ~40 min on gpt2 on two CPU threads.

**Decision: Whether the _meta_ token cloud runs (parameter `token_cloud`).** Default: auto: only when CUDA is available.
Alternatives: true; false.
Recorded in every run: `_meta_token_cloud`.
Why: The token cloud is a _meta_ diagnostic (TwoNN on 20000 token rows per layer): 66 s for the whole task on a GPU, but the CPU ball tree took over 42 min on gpt2 and the 600 s task_timeout discarded the headlines with it.

### `geometry_lid`

Computes per-sample Local Intrinsic Dimensionality (LID) using the **Ma et al. 2018 Eq. (4)** MLE — the ``-k`` (biased) variant,

**Protocol.** LID is Ma et al.'s (2018, Eq. 4) maximum-likelihood local intrinsic dimension, -k / sum_i log(r_i/r_k) over the k=20 nearest neighbours, derived from Levina & Bickel (2004). BLME applies it to one point per sequence, the last token of a 20-word window of each of 1000 passages, as Yin et al. (2024) do with one last-token vector per sample and neighbours drawn from the other samples. The estimate falls as the reference set grows, so N is pinned at 1000 documents for every model rather than set by the tokenizer's token count. Exact-duplicate neighbours give a 0.0 estimate that is kept in the mean, as in the released mle_batch, which is why the minimum is _meta_. The token-cloud value (every token of 200 documents, position 0 and massive rows excluded, 20000 rows) is kept as _meta_tokens_*; there 27-83% of nearest neighbours lie in the token's own document.

**Headline keys.** `lid_mean`, `lid_std`, `lid_max`, `lid_median`, `lid_first_layer`, `lid_mid_layer`.

**References.** paper: Ma et al. 2018 Eq. 4 (-k MLE variant); derived from Levina & Bickel 2004; code: `xingjunm/lid_adversarial_subspace_detection@29ae6f5d1b376753876c9cb404e7fe297d17a2d4`; LLM precedent: Yin, Srinivasa, Chang 2024 arXiv:2402.18048 (one last-token vector per sample, neighbours from the other samples).

**Tokens.** `last_per_sequence`.

**Verification.**

- Kernel: Released code executed on the same inputs. Authors’ mle_batch runs on shared neighbour distances including duplicates. Uses the Ma minus-k convention. Levina–Bickel uses a different finite-k factor.
- Pipeline: match (exact vs released mle_batch); lid_mean changed meaning with layerwise (fixed: layerwise default) (batchC, 2026-11-19, library 1ae4105).
- Next scientific check: Report finite-k sensitivity and the effect of tied or duplicate distances.

**Parameters (shipped defaults).** `k: 20`, `layerwise: True`, `num_samples: 1000`, `token_cloud_docs: 200`, `token_cloud_points: 20000`.

**Decision: Layerwise or single-layer mode (parameter `layerwise`).** Default: true: every block output, the deepest the raw block output; lid_mean/std/max/median are the deepest layer's.
Alternatives: false: HF's post-norm output only (lid_first_layer and lid_mid_layer absent).
Recorded in every run: `_meta_lid_mode`.

### `geometry_weight_norms`

**Protocol.** Each transformer block's weight matrices are summarised by their Frobenius norm, largest singular value and stable rank, averaged within the block and then over blocks; norm_uniformity is one minus the coefficient of variation of the per-block Frobenius norms. ### `topology_betti_curve`

**Protocol.** Naitzat et al. compute Betti numbers of one class's point cloud at every layer of small trained classifiers, on the hop metric of a kNN graph, with k and the radius calibrated so that the input cloud reproduces its known topology. ### `dynamics_gradient_flow`

Per-layer gradient norm profile.

**Protocol.** Pascanu et al. (2013) study how the training-loss gradient shrinks or grows as it is propagated back through time in an RNN; BLME maps time to depth. It backpropagates the model's own next-token cross-entropy on held-out text and records ||dL/dh||_F, over every position but the sink rows, at the input of every block and at the final residual (the input of the final norm). Activation gradients are used instead of the parameter-gradient norms of Xiong et al. (2020), because they are defined for a frozen pretrained model and do not depend on block width or parameterisation; Jacobian spectra are too costly at 34B. The headline is the end-to-end gain log(||g_emb|| / ||g_final||), which identity blocks anywhere and a common residual rescale leave unchanged; entropy, slope, vanishing ratio and raw norms move under one of them and are _meta_. The backward pass runs in fp32 arithmetic, because bf16 doubles the slope on pythia-160m.

**Headline keys.** `gradient_flow_log_ratio_in_out`.

**References.** paper: Pascanu et al. 2013 (ICML, arXiv:1211.5063); LLM precedent: Xiong et al. 2020 (arXiv:2002.04745) Sec. 3.4: per-layer gradient norms vs depth in Pre-/Post-LN Transformers.

### `dynamics_interpolation`

Interpolates between two hidden states in latent space. Measures entropy of decoded predictions along the path (convexity check). Uses norm-corrected linear interpolation and slerp for comparison. Contexts are cut at ``max_tokens`` tokens (default 128). By default every distinct pair of the ``num_samples`` passages is scored (n(n-1)/2 pairs, ``_meta_pair_selection = "all"``); an integer ``num_pairs`` draws that many distinct pairs with the seeded sampler (``"sampled"``).

**Protocol.** convexity_gap asks how much more uncertain the model's next-token distribution becomes midway between two contexts' final states than at the contexts themselves. BLME cuts each context at max_tokens tokens, interpolates the post-final-norm last-token states of two passages linearly, rescales each point to the linearly interpolated norm, decodes it through the model's own LM head, and reports H(alpha=0.5) - mean(H(0), H(1)). The norm correction avoids a mechanical midpoint norm collapse, which would raise entropy for geometric reasons alone, and post-norm states are used because the head only ever sees post-norm vectors. Slerp (White 2016) was the alternative path; with the same norm target it is identical at alpha in {0, 0.5, 1}, so it is kept only as a _meta_ comparison. The endpoint entropies are the model's own predictive entropy, so the gap contains a capability term at coefficient -1, disclosed in _meta_convexity_gap_endpoint_entropy_mean. The forward pass and the decode run in fp32 arithmetic.

**Headline keys.** `convexity_gap`.

**References.** paper: BLME convexity proxy; slerp kernel = White 2016 (arXiv:1609.04468); code: `dribnet/plat@0963c75d460c153183c1d414b02d4b5dc0b2208f`.

**Tokens.** `not_token_based`.

**Verification.**

- Kernel: Equations and analytic cases (no external reference exists). Known midpoint entropies check the convexity gap. The reference for SLERP covers an interpolation primitive. The sampled layer readout and performance interpretation are study choices.
- Pipeline: match (all 1225 pairs) (batchB, 2026-11-19, library fbce8b8).
- Next scientific check: Vary layer pairs and sequence samples and quantify resulting changes in convexity gap.

**Parameters (shipped defaults).** `num_pairs: null`, `num_samples: 10`, `steps: 10`.

**Decision: Which context pairs are scored (parameter `num_pairs`).** Default: null: every distinct pair of the num_samples pool, canonical order.
Alternatives: an integer: that many distinct pairs from the seeded sampler.
Recorded in every run: `_meta_pair_selection`, `_meta_num_pairs_used`, `_meta_num_pairs_requested`.

### `dynamics_perturbation_growth`

Seeded input-embedding perturbation; per-layer divergence growth.

**Protocol.** The quasi-Lyapunov exponent of Li et al. (2025) perturbs one element of one intermediate-layer row and tracks per-position divergence. BLME instead perturbs every input embedding by a seeded isotropic direction of norm rel_eps * ||e_t|| and tracks the token-mean divergence (sink rows excluded) through every block, which costs two forward passes per passage; per-element QLE would need d x T x L passes and Jacobian spectra are infeasible at 34B. The headline is the end-to-end relative amplification growth_log_ratio_relative = log(r_L / r_0), r_l the token mean of ||h_l - h'_l|| / ||h_l||, which identity-block insertion leaves bit-identical; the absolute log(d_L / d_0) mostly tracks residual-norm growth and is _meta_, as are the per-block rate and the normalised-depth slope. growth_log_ratio_relative is a BLME formulation, not the quasi-Lyapunov exponent: Li et al.'s Eq. 9 is lambda = 1/(n - m) * ln(||delta_{m,n}|| / |delta_m|), a per-layer rate with no state-norm normalisation. Its closest analogue here is _meta_growth_rate_per_block = log(d_L / d_0) / L, which keeps BLME's all-token perturbation and token-mean divergence. The forward passes run in fp32 arithmetic, because bf16 rounding is a noise floor of the same order as the perturbation.

**Headline keys.** `growth_log_ratio_relative`.

**References.** paper: Li et al. 2025 (arXiv:2503.13530, quasi-Lyapunov grounding); Fernando & Guitchounts 2025/26 (residual-stream dynamics context); LLM precedent: Li et al. 2025 (arXiv:2503.13530): quasi-Lyapunov exponent of a perturbation propagated through LLaMA-family layers.

**Tokens.** `exclude_sinks`.

**Verification.**

- Kernel: BLME proxy: an operational definition of its own. Known contracting and expanding systems check finite-depth divergence under seeded embedding perturbations. The headline is a BLME diagnostic. It does not implement the quoted Lyapunov experiment. For models with per-layer token embeddings, both forwards use the same token-identity lookup while the main embedding receives the specified perturbation.
- Pipeline: match (same seed-42 draw) (batchB, 2026-11-19, library fbce8b8).
- Next scientific check: Repeat seeded perturbations over step sizes and known contracting and expanding dynamics.

**Parameters (shipped defaults).** `num_samples: 10`, `rel_eps: 0.01`, `seed: 42`.

### `dynamics_stability`

Measures Jaccard stability of k-NN neighborhoods. If 'reference_model_path' is provided in config, compares current model to reference. Otherwise, compares the embedding neighborhood graph against a seeded small Gaussian perturbation of the embeddings.

**Protocol.** Wendlandt et al. define stability as the overlap of a word's ten nearest cosine neighbours across independently trained embedding spaces. Released LLMs have no sibling training runs, so BLME instead perturbs each input-embedding row by a seeded isotropic direction of 1% of its norm and reports the mean Jaccard of the k = 50 nearest-neighbour sets before and after, over sampled rows the tokenizer can emit. This measures how much margin separates a token's neighbourhood boundary from its runners-up, a local robustness property of the embedding graph. Alternatives were base-vs-instruct embeddings (paired models only) and per-coordinate noise (whose realised size grows as sqrt(d)); fixed-norm noise realises the requested 1% at every width. Two mechanical size dependences remain: an isometric re-embedding into 2x/4x width raises the value (0.950 -> 0.963 -> 0.973), and it depends on how many emittable rows form the neighbourhoods.

**Headline keys.** `stability_mean`.

**References.** paper: BLME embedding-neighborhood diagnostic; inspired by Wendlandt et al. 2018 (arXiv:1804.09692).

**Tokens.** `not_token_based`.

**Verification.**

- Kernel: BLME proxy: an operational definition of its own. Jaccard overlap on perturbed embeddings tests local sensitivity within one model. Retraining embeddings on different corpora would measure a different stability construct.
- Pipeline: match (within the seed spread) (batchB, 2026-11-19, library fbce8b8).
- Next scientific check: Compare local perturbation overlap with overlap across independently trained embeddings.

**Parameters (shipped defaults).** `k: 50`, `num_samples: 5000`, `reference_model_path: null`.

### `repe_steering_effectiveness`

Effect of adding the contrastive task vector to the residual stream.

**Protocol.** Steering effectiveness is a BLME construct. The last-token mean difference of eight truthful/untruthful sentence pairs is rescaled to 0.1 times the norm of the residual it is added to (the relative norm of ActAdd App. F; ActAdd and CAA sweep a raw multiplier instead) and added at the last position of a neutral prompt.
